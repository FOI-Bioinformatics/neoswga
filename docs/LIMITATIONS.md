# What NeoSWGA does not establish

Read this before trusting a number from a design.

This project keeps 43 measurement records under `docs/validation/`, and most of
them exist because a plausible-looking figure turned out to describe something
other than what a reader would assume. This page collects what those records
say a design run does **not** tell you. It is not a list of bugs; every item
here is current, deliberate behaviour.

An external comparative review in September 2026 put the general point well:
the framework is broad, and the evidence does not establish that it produces
the best experimentally performing pools. That remains true.

## The headline: no design from this tool has been tested in a laboratory

There is no prospective validation. No pool this tool designed has been
synthesised, run and sequenced as part of its development, and no head-to-head
against SWGA, SOAPswga or COATswga has been performed.

What exists instead is retrospective: published primer sets with known outcomes
are scored, and the tool's own metrics are checked against independent
oracles. That establishes internal consistency, not biological performance.

**So "coverage 0.85" is a modelled property of a primer set, not a prediction
that 85% of the genome will be sequenced.**

## Coverage is a geometric proxy

`fg_coverage` is the fraction of target bases within reach of a binding site,
at a chosen per-primer reach. Three things follow.

**The reach is a modelling choice, not a measurement.** Selection and scoring
use roughly 3 kb for phi29, while the same enzyme's single-molecule
processivity is about 70 kb. Neither has been calibrated against a reaction,
because there is no sequencing data in this repository to calibrate against.

**Contributions are combined under an independence approximation.** Two primers
binding near each other are assumed to cover independently.

**Two of the code's own coverage paths disagree across a contig join**, by up
to 4.8% on a fragmented assembly. Measured and deliberately not yet unified;
see [record_geometry_on_drosophila_2026-09-21.md](validation/record_geometry_on_drosophila_2026-09-21.md).

## Specificity barely uses where the host sites are

`selectivity_density` and `total_bg_sites` are additive over per-primer counts.
No position enters. Two backgrounds with identical per-primer counts therefore
score identically whether their sites are clustered or dispersed — and
clustering is most of what drives off-target amplification, because
amplification needs two convergent sites within the polymerase's reach.

Only `bg_coverage` sees position, and nothing selects on it.

## Nothing here is fitted to measured amplification

The bundled random forest was retired from the default path in September 2026.
It was fitted to synthetic data generated by a hand-written rule, so it
reproduced an opinion rather than an outcome, and taking the top half of a pool
by its score produced a worse design than three random halves.

`--amp-model` restores it. It is not evidence.

## The chemistry model is mostly extrapolated, and says so

`neoswga/core/registry/model_evidence.json` records, per constant, whether it is
`measured`, `estimated`, `empirical`, `assumed` or `absent`. Most additive
coefficients are 37 C figures for PCR-length duplexes applied to 12-mers at
30 C. That may be fine; it is not a measurement of it.

**The registry does not claim the literature was re-read.** Every source is the
attribution the repository already carried; what the registry adds is the
judgement of whether the cited work covers this case.

Consequently only **one of twenty-three** records states a temperature range
that can be checked mechanically. Ten give a single reference point, twelve give
no number. A range is enforced where one is recorded and invented nowhere.

Some additives change no melting temperature at all. Glycerol is the clearest:
a design that sets it is refused rather than given an invented coefficient. BSA
and PEG have no duplex term because they act on the enzyme and on crowding.

## Thermodynamic discrimination is largely unavailable at the default reaction

At phi29 and 30 C, 86% of random 12-mers are bound essentially all the time, so
occupancy separates almost nothing. Specificity in that regime is a property of
where sites fall, not of how tightly anything binds.

The lever that changes this is the reaction, not the candidate filter: an
occupancy gate was measured and made delivered panels worse on both axes. See
[occupancy_and_discrimination_2026-09-19.md](validation/occupancy_and_discrimination_2026-09-19.md).

## Spacing statistics are reported and never ranked

`max_gap`, mean gap and gap evenness reach the summary and the reports, and no
optimizer scores them. That is deliberate: no threshold derived from the
polymerase reach separates the 18 published sets with wet-lab outcomes, the
winners included, so the tool will not pick one.

You can impose your own through the panel limits. The "what limits this panel"
table names which properties had no reference at all.

## The search examines a fraction of what it could

A run that qualifies on its opening frontier stops there. On one measured
design that was 2,000 of 365,073 candidates that cleared quality control.

This is a deliberate default, and looking further was measured to be worse:
coverage rises 0.3–1.5% relative while selectivity density falls 9–26%, at four
times the runtime. The candidates a wider search reaches are by construction
the ones that bind the host more, and the first stage has no specificity term
to resist them. See
[looking_further_costs_specificity_2026-09-22.md](validation/looking_further_costs_specificity_2026-09-22.md).

**The tool does not claim to have found the smallest or best pool.** Where a
size is described as smallest, it means smallest among those evaluated.

## The panel size you ask for is a request

The delivered panel is never larger and may be smaller. Selection stops rather
than admitting a pair above `max_dimer_bp`, and the first stage stops once the
coverage target is met. A short panel is reported with its reason.

## Where a number is absent rather than zero

A great deal of this project's effort has gone into keeping "not measured"
distinguishable from "measured zero". Strand statistics read `None` rather than
0.0 when they cannot be computed; a verified zero background is reported as an
undefined ratio rather than infinite selectivity; a required calculation that
fails fails the run instead of returning a substitute.

If you see a figure, it was computed. If you see nothing, nothing was.

## How to read a claim in this repository

Each validation record states its own caveats, and they are usually narrow: one
organism pair, one reaction, one panel size, one seed. A result measured with
constraints in force says nothing about a path that has none.

Where this project has been wrong, the record says so in place rather than
being quietly corrected — including several claims withdrawn after a second
measurement contradicted the first.
